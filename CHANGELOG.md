# Changes

## Unreleased

- The library: `play_sound`, `play_note`, `play_notes`, `make_synth`, `load_sample`, `play_song`, `stop_sound`, `stop_song`, `set_volume`, `mute_sound`, `unmute_sound`, `set_effects`, `set_tempo`, `beats`, `beat` and `sound_time`. The same names as synths.js, in Python's spelling.
- A synth's settings are plain attributes (`lead.cutoff = 800`, `lead.vibrato_rate = 6`) that read back what was set. `lead.play`, `lead.start` (a note that stays, and can slide), `lead.stop`, `lead.play_notes`, `lead.set(cutoff=4000, slide=2)` and `lead.remove`.
- Chords by name (`lead.play("Cm7")`, `chord_notes("C3", "minor")`), arpeggios (`lead.arp("Cm7")`, `arp = lead.start_arp("Am")` with `arp.change("F")`, `play_arp`, `start_arp`) and step macros (`make_synth("chip", macro=[…])`, `lead.macro = …`).
- Songs: `song = play_song("tune.mod")` plays a `.mod`, an `.xm` or a song in synths' own format, with `pause`, `resume`, `seek`, `stop`, `song.volume` and `song.repeat`, and `patches=` for a song's instruments that are synths by name.
- Musical time: a length may be a note length by name (`"quarter"`, `"eighth."`, `"1/16"`, a triplet), measured against `set_tempo`, wherever a length is taken, and a number is still seconds. `note`, `chord` and `rest` write a tune one line a note, each beginning where the last ended, on any synth; lines written together start together; `with loop_notes():` has a section go round, and `start_lines()` begins again. A tune with no step, or a step by name, follows the tempo as it plays.
- MIDI, as synths.js has it, in Python's spelling. `lead.play_from_midi()` has a MIDI keyboard play a synth and `lead.learn("cutoff")` or `lead.map_midi(74, "cutoff")` has a knob turn a setting, both done by the page itself so they are immediate however busy or far away Python is; `lead.cutoff` reads what the knob set. `on_midi_note`, `on_midi_note_off`, `on_midi_control`, `on_midi` and `on_midi_devices` call your functions with what arrived (once a frame, each message with the time it happened), and in a Pyctures scene a function of your own with one of those names is called with nothing more to write. `send_midi_note("C4", 0.8, length="quarter")`, `send_midi_note_off`, `send_midi_control`, `send_midi_bend`, `send_midi_program`, `send_midi` and `send_midi_clock` send, with `after=`, `at=`, `beat=`, `channel=` and `output=`; `lead.send_to_midi()` sends what a synth plays. `midi_devices()`, `use_midi_input`, `use_midi_output`, `receive_midi` and `stop_midi`. Where there is no MIDI it is said once and the program carries on.
- The microphone, as synths.js has it, in Python's spelling. `mic = use_microphone()` is a synth whose sound comes in: `mic.voice = "robot"` (robot, chipmunk, monster, cave, telephone, alien, whisper), `mic.listen()` and `mic.mute()`, `mic.pitch`, `mic.ring`, `mic.comb`, `mic.gain`, `mic.gate` and every setting a synth has; `mic.state`, `mic.ready`, `mic.on_ready(fn)`; `stop_microphone()`. It is not heard until `listen()`. The page opens it, and a dial tied to it acts on it there.
- Recording. `take = record_sound(3)`, `start_recording()` and `stop_recording()` record the microphone into a sample that is given back at once (`take.done`, `take.seconds`, `take.on_done(fn)`, `await take.when_done()`), plays like any sample (`take.play("E4")`, `take.reverse`, `take.loop`) and wears the microphone's voice (`take.voice`, `take.sound_like(mic)`). `record_output(10)` records everything that is played, and `lead.record(4)` one synth.
- `save_sound(take, "hello.wav")` saves a WAV file: beside the program under `pyctures run`, downloaded or kept by the page otherwise, and `download=False` hands Python the bytes.
- The voice effects on every synth: `lead.pitch_shift`, `lead.ring_mod`, `lead.ring_speed`, `lead.comb`, `lead.comb_tone`, `lead.flanger`, and `lead.voice = "robot"`. `voice_names()`.
- A song's `mute`, `unmute`, `solo`, `loop_pattern`, `replace`, `channel(n)` and `position`.
- Reading the sound back, for a program that draws it: `lead.samples()`, `lead.level()` and `lead.notes()`, the same on `sound_output()`, the microphone and a song's channels. What the page says arrives with the next frame, so these, `take.done`, `mic.state` and `song.position` are a frame late; nothing waits for them.
- `lead.set(cutoff=4000, glide=2)`: `glide` is the word for sliding a setting, and `slide` still works.
- With no microphone nothing is raised: the page says why once, and a recording is empty.
- A sample as the wave of any synth: `lead.sample = load_sample("piano.wav")`, or `make_synth(sample="piano.wav", note="C4")`, which loads the file. The wave `"sample"` is offered only for a synth that has one.
- `midi_input()`, `lead.midi_input()` and `midi_output()`: the MIDI device and channel as a target with `"device"` and `"channel"`, for a select on the screen.
- The reverb has types: `set_effects(reverb_type="hall")` (room, hall, plate, cathedral, spring), `reverb_decay`, `reverb_predelay`, `reverb_low_cut`. A setting every synth shares has one value, whoever set it, and every synth and `sound_output()` read that back.
- `pwm` and `pwm_rate`: a pulse wave's width moving by itself, and how fast.
- `set_sound_buffer("large")` and `sound_buffer()`, as synths.js 932eeab has them: how much sound is made at a time on a page with no https address, set by a word or a number of frames, and read back as frames, delay, thread, under-runs and setting.
- `synths.receive_sound_events(events)` hands a program what a host's player said back.
- `after=`, `at=` and `beat=` on everything that makes a sound.
- Mistakes are said in plain words, with what was probably meant: a sound, synth, setting, choice, note, chord or order that does not exist.
- The names of every sound, ready-made synth and setting are those of synths.js, carried inside the one module by `scripts/sync_names.py`.
- In a Pyctures scene a frame's sounds travel as one command in the frame's own message, timed on Python's clock. A synth's `page` is the page's own object for it, so dials and piano keys made with pydials act on it at once. `main_synth()` and `sound_output()` can be tied to dials too.
- `synths.connect(send)` hands every command to a host.
- A program started with plain Python is started again with `pyctures run` when Pyctures is installed, and otherwise says how to run it and carries on in silence.
